# CPH 705 — Public Health Informatics

## Course Overview
Advanced study of public health surveillance systems, data standards, and informatics applications in public health practice.

## Key Projects & Assignments

### Needs Assessment — Cancer Surveillance Training Module

**Target Audience**: 
- Graduate public health students
- Oncology fellows  
- Research professionals working with cancer surveillance data

**Key Learner Characteristics**:
- Mixed backgrounds (clinical vs. data science)
- Need hands-on, case-based learning
- Require training tied to real workflows and coding standards

**Data Collection Strategy**:

**Existing Data Sources**:
- Course evaluations and feedback
- Direct observations of learner challenges
- Survey results and activity logs
- Feedback from registry liaisons
- Analysis of related course syllabi

**New Data Collection**:
- Pre- and post-training surveys
- Structured interviews with stakeholders
- Focus groups with target learners
- Direct observation of skill application
- Practical skill assessment checks

**Collection Methods**:
- Online survey forms and assessments
- Guided interviews and focus groups (Zoom/Teams with transcription)
- Hands-on lab skill verification
- Miro boards for collaborative input
- Airtable for tracking progress and competencies

**Analysis Plan**:
- Descriptive statistics for survey responses
- Paired t-tests and chi-square analysis for pre/post comparisons
- Interactive dashboards (Tableau/Google Data Studio) for visualization
- Thematic analysis of interview and focus group transcripts
- Benchmarking against prior cohort performance

**Dissemination & Implementation**:
- Comprehensive reports with data visualizations
- Presentation slide decks for stakeholders
- Interactive dashboards for ongoing monitoring
- Targeted email communications
- Stakeholder meetings for feedback integration
- Sample implementation plans
- Continuous improvement cycles

### Backward Design — Introduction to Public Health Surveillance Systems (Cancer/Lymphoma Focus)

**Learning Outcomes**:
- Explain core goals and purposes of public health surveillance systems
- Identify key data collection processes and coding standards
- Describe lymphoma-specific registry data capture and management
- Analyze cancer incidence and survival trends using surveillance data
- Evaluate how surveillance findings inform research, policy, and patient care

**Assessment Evidence**:
- Data interpretation and analysis quizzes
- Case study analysis assignments
- Policy brief development exercises
- Peer review of scientific articles
- Reflective journal entries on learning process

**Learning Activities**:
- Lectures on registry systems and data sources
- Hands-on laboratory sessions with public health portals (CDC, SEER, OnCore)
- Small group analysis of surveillance reports
- Guest lectures from practicing registry epidemiologists
- Policy brief development workshops
- Data quality assessment exercises

## Course Reflections
*This course provided practical experience in designing public health training programs and understanding the complexities of cancer surveillance systems. The backward design approach ensured alignment between learning objectives, assessments, and instructional activities.*
